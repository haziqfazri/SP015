Learning outcomes — 8.3(a–c)

Heat conduction transfers energy through a temperature gradient. For insulated rods in steady one-dimensional conduction, thermal resistances add in series.

Heat-rate equations

What to notice

The heat rate is the same through rods in series. The larger thermal resistance produces the larger temperature drop. With +x from hot to cold, the temperature gradient is negative while heat flow is positive.

Schematic side loss

Heat flow varies along exposed sections, so their temperature–distance graph is curved. An insulated section remains linear. Conductivity changes this qualitative steepness only; without ambient and surface-loss data, interface temperatures and heat rates are not calculated.
