Learning outcomes — 8.4(a–b)

Define the coefficients of linear, area, and volume expansion, then solve thermal-expansion problems including a liquid in a container.

Thermal expansion equations

Linear expansion

A fixed origin makes the signed length change easy to see. Heating gives positive expansion; cooling gives contraction.

Area coefficient

For an isotropic solid in the first-order model, both in-plane dimensions change and beta equals two alpha.

Volume coefficient

All three dimensions change, giving gamma equals three alpha in the first-order model.

Liquid in a container

The liquid and container both expand. Apparent expansion depends on the difference between their gamma values; overflow occurs only above the expanded capacity.
