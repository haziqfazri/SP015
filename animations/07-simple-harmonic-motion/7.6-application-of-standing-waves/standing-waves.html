Learning Outcome — 7.6(a.i), 7.6(b)

A string fixed at both ends supports standing waves only at frequencies where both ends are displacement nodes. Wave speed depends on the string's tension and mass per unit length.

fn = nv / 2L    v = √(T/μ)

Learning Outcome — 7.6(a.ii)

An air column open at both ends supports standing waves where both ends are displacement antinodes, so every harmonic (n = 1, 2, 3, ...) is possible — the same frequency pattern as the fixed-fixed string.

fn = nv / 2L    v = 343 m/s

Learning Outcome — 7.6(a.ii)

An air column closed at one end has a displacement node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end. This boundary condition only fits odd-numbered harmonics — even harmonics cannot exist.

fn = nv / 4L   (n = 1, 3, 5, ...)

What to notice

Higher tension or lower linear density increases wave speed, which raises every harmonic's frequency together — the harmonic pattern (node/antinode spacing) itself doesn't change, only the pitch.

What to notice

Because both ends are antinodes, the open column's harmonic series matches the stretched string's exactly — same formula, same allowed n values, just a different physical system.

What to notice

The 2nd overtone of a closed column is the 5th harmonic (n = 5), not the 3rd — overtone numbering always counts upward from the fundamental among only the frequencies that actually exist.
