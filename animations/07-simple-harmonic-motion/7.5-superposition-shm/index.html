Learning Outcome — 7.5(a)

State the principle of superposition of waves for constructive and destructive interference: when two waves meet, the resultant displacement is the sum of each wave's individual displacement.

y_resultant = y_A + y_B

Learning Outcomes — 7.5(a), 7.5(c)

Two identical progressive waves combine by the same superposition rule as the pulse mode. Their direction relationship and phase difference determine whether the continuous result travels or forms a standing pattern.

y_resultant = y_A + y_B

What to notice

Each pulse keeps its own shape and amplitude before and after overlap — superposition is a momentary addition, not a permanent change to either wave.

Key Equations

y_A = A sin(ωt − kx)
y_B = A sin(ωt − kx + Δφ)  |  k = 2π/λ

With both waves travelling in +x, the resultant remains a travelling wave. Δφ = 0 doubles the amplitude and Δφ = π cancels it everywhere.

y_R = 2A cos(Δφ/2) × sin(ωt − kx + Δφ/2)
